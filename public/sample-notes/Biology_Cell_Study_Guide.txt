Cell Biology and Cellular Respiration Study Guide

Cell Membrane: A phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins that regulates the movement of substances entering and exiting the cell.
Mitochondria: Double-membraned organelles known as the powerhouse of the cell that generate ATP through oxidative phosphorylation.
Ribosomes: Molecular complexes composed of ribosomal RNA and proteins that synthesize polypeptides by translating mRNA transcripts.
Endoplasmic Reticulum: An extensive network of membranous tubules; the rough ER synthesizes proteins while the smooth ER synthesizes lipids and detoxifies toxins.
Golgi Apparatus: Flattened membrane-bound cisternae responsible for modifying, sorting, and packaging proteins for secretion or cellular delivery.
Lysosomes: Spherical vesicles containing hydrolytic acid enzymes that degrade cellular waste, foreign debris, and damaged organelles.

The primary function of Chloroplasts is to conduct photosynthesis by converting solar energy, carbon dioxide, and water into chemical glucose.
Active transport refers to the cellular movement of solutes against their concentration gradient requiring ATP hydrolysis.
Osmosis is defined as the net movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from an area of higher water potential to lower water potential.

Mitosis is divided into four distinct phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase.
During Metaphase, chromosomes align along the equatorial metaphase plate attached to spindle microtubules at their kinetochores.
During Anaphase, sister chromatids separate at their centromeres and are pulled toward opposite cellular poles.
